Subtopic 3

Interview Preparation

How to prepare for technical and behavioural questions and explain your work with confidence.

01 • Core Guide

Preparing for your interview

Good interview performance is mostly preparation. If you know the employer, the role and your own projects well, you can answer calmly even when a question is unexpected.

Before the interview

Research the employer

Read their website and recent news. Understand what they do, who their customers are and which technologies the job advert mentions.

Match the job advert

List the main requirements and write one example from your studies or projects for each. These become your answers.

Revise and practise

Review the topics on your CV, then practise answering out loud with a friend, lecturer or even a mirror.

Technical questions

For ICT and cybersecurity roles, expect questions on fundamentals. Interviewers usually care more about how you reason than whether you remember every detail. If you do not know, say so, then explain how you would find out. Select a question to see what a good answer covers.

Confidentiality (only authorised people can see data), integrity (data is accurate and not changed without permission) and availability (systems and data can be used when needed). A strong answer gives one example of each, such as encryption, hashing or backups.

TCP is connection-oriented and reliable: it sets up a connection, confirms delivery and keeps data in order. UDP is connectionless and faster with less overhead, but gives no delivery guarantee. Mention a use for each, for example web browsing for TCP and live video or voice for UDP.

Encryption is two-way: data is scrambled with a key and can be turned back into the original. Hashing is one-way: it produces a fixed-length fingerprint that cannot be reversed. Hashing suits password storage and integrity checks, while encryption suits protecting data in transit or at rest.

Tell them not to click links or open attachments, and ask for the email to be forwarded or reported through the proper channel. Check the sender, links and headers, then follow the organisation's incident process: record it, block or remove the message if confirmed malicious and check whether anyone else received it or clicked.

Behavioural questions and the STAR method

Behavioural questions begin with phrases like "Tell me about a time when...". They test how you work with others and handle problems. The STAR method keeps your answer structured:

Situation

Briefly set the scene.

Task

What you were responsible for.

Action

What you did, step by step.

Result

The outcome and what you learned.

Spend most of your time on the Action. Use "I" for your own contribution, and be honest about what the team did.

Questions to ask the interviewer

Almost every interview ends with "Do you have any questions for us?" Have two or three ready. Good examples:

  • What would a typical first month in this role look like?
  • Which tools and technologies does the team use day to day?
  • How does the team support new graduates or interns to learn?
  • What are the next steps in the selection process?

Examples and practical tips

Example STAR answer

Question: Tell me about a time you solved a problem in a team project.

Situation: In a four-person web project, our contact form stopped working two days before the deadline.

Task: I was responsible for the JavaScript validation, so I needed to find the cause quickly.

Action: I used the browser developer tools to find an error caused by a mismatched element ID, fixed it and tested each field with valid and invalid input. I then added a short test checklist to our shared document so that the team could re-check after changes.

Result: The form worked again the same day, and the checklist helped us catch two more small bugs before submitting. I learned to test after every change, not just at the end.

Before

  • Confirm the date, time, place or video link.
  • Print or save copies of your CV and have a notebook ready.
  • Plan your journey and aim to arrive early.
  • For online interviews, test your camera, microphone and internet, and pick a quiet place.

During

  • Dress smartly and greet everyone politely.
  • Listen to the whole question and pause to think.
  • Give examples from your own work rather than general statements.
  • Be honest. If you do not know something, explain how you would find out.

After

  • Send a short thank-you email within a day.
  • Write down the questions you were asked and how you answered.
  • Use what you learned to improve for the next interview, whatever the result.
