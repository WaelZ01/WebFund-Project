AI Documentation & Reflection

Which AI tools we used, what we learned, and how we worked as a team.

AI utilisation report

Our team used three AI tools. We used them as assistants for planning ideas, wording, code suggestions and checking. A team member read and tested every suggestion before it was used.

AI tool Developer Used for
ClaudeAnthropicDocumentation, testing and drafting
GeminiGooglePlanning ideas and checking
ChatGPTOpenAIWording and code suggestions

We asked four questions of every suggestion. Is it accurate? Does it suit students in Bahrain? Does it meet the brief? Is the final version our own work? Anything that failed these checks was rewritten or discarded.

Prompts, outputs and our changes are recorded in the AI documentation table on the Overview page.

Project reflection

Career Prep Hub gives ICT and Cybersecurity students at Bahrain Polytechnic practical guidance on career paths, CVs and interviews. We built seven pages with HTML5, CSS3, Bootstrap 5 and JavaScript, and we aimed for a site that real students could use, not only an assessment. The project showed us that a useful website starts with a clear problem: students find career advice that is generic and not written for Bahrain.

Our design changed several times. We moved from a Nordic Ocean palette to a bento layout and finally to the Terrazzo theme with a floating navigation bar. Keeping colours in CSS variables meant each change took minutes rather than hours.

The registration form is our strongest feature. It validates in the browser, shows a message beside each field and accepts only digits in the mobile field. The CV and interview pages also include interactive tools, which make the site more useful than static text.

Lessons learned

  • AI output is a draft. Suggestions can be generic or wrong, so we checked each one against our audience and the brief.
  • Test, do not assume. We tried short names, 7 and 9 digit numbers and badly formed emails in the registration form, then recorded the results on the Planning & Design page.
  • Accessibility needs measuring. Our contrast check showed white text on the orange buttons reaches only 3.08:1, below the 4.5:1 target, so a darker orange is our first improvement.
  • Plan first. Wireframes and the five-layer plan gave us a shared target, so even when the visual theme changed, the page structure stayed the same.

Team collaboration

We divided the pages between members and used a shared Git repository. Small commits with clear messages let us see what changed and why. The hardest part was keeping styling consistent while several people edited, and a single stylesheet and shared navigation bar solved most of it. Working in parallel also taught us to agree on file names and shared components early, as when we aligned our file names with the brief.

Ethical considerations

We disclose our AI use, check facts before publishing them, and credit our sources. We did not enter personal data into AI tools, and we label AI-assisted material in our documentation so readers can tell it apart from our own writing. The registration form is a demonstration only and stores nothing.

What we would do differently

We would keep a prompt log from the first day so that documentation is complete and accurate. We would also test earlier on real phones and fix the button contrast sooner. Finally, we would ask real students for feedback before finalising the content.
